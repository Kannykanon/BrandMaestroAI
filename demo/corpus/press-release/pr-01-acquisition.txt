Harbor Line Pictures Takes Worldwide Rights to SALVAGE

Ines Kowalczyk's first narrative feature premieres in Competition at Cascadia on 14 October

LONDON — The tank held four hundred thousand litres. The crew filled it once, in March, and left it full for eleven weeks.

Harbor Line Pictures has acquired worldwide rights to SALVAGE, the debut narrative feature from documentary director Ines Kowalczyk. The film premieres in Competition at the Cascadia International Film Festival on 14 October and opens in cinemas on 6 February.

SALVAGE follows three commercial divers cutting apart a sunken cargo ship in the Irish Sea. They have sixteen days of workable weather and a contract that pays on tonnage. The ship's hold is not empty. What they find in it is worth more than the steel, and worth more than the three of them can agree on.

Tobi Adeyemi plays Wale, the crew's supervisor. Marta Silvestri plays Renata, a diver who's eight months out of a decompression injury. Cillian Rourke plays Sam, who is twenty-three and has never worked a wreck.

Kowalczyk spent two years on North Sea vessels making her 2022 documentary Ninety Fathoms. She'd written SALVAGE with Daniel Achebe during that film's edit.

"I kept hearing the same thing from divers, which is that the water isn't the frightening part," said Kowalczyk. "The frightening part is the surface. Somebody up there is deciding how long you stay down, and they're doing it with a spreadsheet."

The production built its tank at a former shipyard in Ardrossan and dressed a wreck interior inside it. Nothing in the film's underwater photography is dry-for-wet. Exterior work was shot aboard the Kestrel, a working salvage barge, over nineteen days on Belfast Lough. The three leads trained for nine weeks with commercial diving instructors before principal photography. Adeyemi and Silvestri perform their own underwater work in every scene they appear in.

There is one shot of digital water in the film. It runs four seconds, and Kowalczyk won't say which one it is.

"We saw this in April with ten minutes of temp sound and we knew by the second reel," said Priya Vellani, head of acquisitions at Harbor Line. "It's a heist film that happens to be about labour. Or it's a labour film that happens to have a heist in it. Ines won't settle the argument, and that's exactly why it works."

SALVAGE is produced by Freight & Tide. Nadia Berhe and Owen Pryce produce. Sung-min Ha is the director of photography. Wren Okpara is the sound designer. Lior Cattan composed the score. The film runs 108 minutes.

Harbor Line will open SALVAGE in limited release on 30 January, with a national expansion on 6 February.

About Harbor Line Pictures

Harbor Line Pictures acquires and distributes narrative features in North America, the United Kingdom and Ireland. The company releases between six and nine films a year.
