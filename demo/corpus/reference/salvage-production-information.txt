Internal reference sheet. Facts for publicity use.

Jury citation, in full: "For a film that trusts its audience to hear what it
  will not show them."

PREMISE

Three commercial divers are contracted to cut apart a sunken cargo ship in the
Irish Sea. The contract pays on tonnage. They have sixteen days of workable
weather. The ship's hold is not empty, and what is in it is worth more than the
steel.

CAST

Tobi Adeyemi as Wale, the crew's supervisor
Marta Silvestri as Renata, a diver eight months after a decompression injury
Cillian Rourke as Sam, twenty-three, on his first wreck

PRODUCTION

Tank: purpose-built at a former shipyard in Ardrossan; four hundred thousand
  litres; filled once in March and not drained for eleven weeks
Wreck interior: dressed inside the tank
Exteriors: nineteen days aboard the Kestrel, a working salvage barge, on
  Belfast Lough
Diver training: nine weeks with commercial diving instructors before principal
  photography
Underwater performance: Adeyemi and Silvestri perform their own underwater work
  in every scene in which they appear
Digital water: one shot, four seconds long. Kowalczyk declines to identify it
Camera: Alexa in underwater housings; wreck interior lit with practical work
  lamps rigged by the diving crew
Sound: recorded live in the tank using hydrophones on the divers' helmets and on
  the hull. Seven months on the mix

BACKGROUND

Kowalczyk's previous film, the documentary Ninety Fathoms (2022), was shot over
two years aboard four North Sea vessels and screened at thirty-one festivals.
SALVAGE is her first narrative feature. She wrote it with Daniel Achebe during
the Ninety Fathoms edit.

Silvestri has made eleven features across four countries and speaks Italian,
English and Portuguese. She learned to work a full face mask in three days and
asked to audition in the tank.

Adeyemi came to acting at twenty-eight after six years fitting shopfronts and
has worked mainly in theatre. SALVAGE is his second feature. He had never dived
before he was cast.

Rourke was cast from a self-tape recorded in his sister's kitchen. SALVAGE is
his first feature.

APPROVED QUOTATIONS

Ines Kowalczyk, director:
"I kept hearing the same thing from divers, which is that the water isn't the
frightening part. The frightening part is the surface. Somebody up there is
deciding how long you stay down, and they're doing it with a spreadsheet."

Ines Kowalczyk, director:
"I've never made anything for a room before. Documentaries get watched on
laptops mostly. Sitting in the dark on Sunday and hearing four hundred people
stop breathing at the same moment — I don't have anything to compare that to."

Wren Okpara, sound designer:
"People assume underwater sound is muffled. It isn't. It's loud, and it's close,
and it's mostly your own body. That was the note for seven months."

Marta Silvestri:
"I didn't want to find out on the day that I couldn't do it. Better to know in
September than in March, with a crew waiting."

Priya Vellani, head of acquisitions, Harbor Line Pictures:
"It's a heist film that happens to be about labour. Or it's a labour film that
happens to have a heist in it. Ines won't settle the argument, and that's
exactly why it works."

INTERNAL NOTES — NOT FOR PUBLICATION

Positioning: lead on craft and labour, not on the discovery in the hold. Reviews
that led on the hold underperformed in testing against reviews that led on the
tank build.

Do not disclose: the budget, the identity of the four-second digital shot, or
anything about the final twenty minutes.

Audience: arthouse core with genre crossover. Second-weekend hold was stronger
than opening in every limited market, so word of mouth is the lever.

Talent availability: Kowalczyk and Okpara for press through April. Silvestri
unavailable after 20 March. Adeyemi will not discuss the decompression sequence.

Rights note: Harbor Line holds worldwide. Do not state territory-by-territory
dates without checking with the distribution team first.
