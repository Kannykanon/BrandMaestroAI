SALVAGE, in Competition at Cascadia.

X — awards

SALVAGE has won the Grand Jury Prize for Direction at Cascadia. Wren Okpara won Best Sound.
The jury citation was one sentence: "For a film that trusts its audience to hear what it will not show them."

INSTAGRAM — second screening

The first Sunday screening sold out in nine minutes, so the festival added another.
Both were full.

X — Kowalczyk

"Documentaries get watched on laptops mostly. Sitting in the dark on Sunday and hearing four hundred people stop breathing at the same moment — I don't have anything to compare that to."

INSTAGRAM — Tobi Adeyemi

Tobi Adeyemi plays Wale, the supervisor. He's the one who decides how long anybody stays down, and he's the one holding the contract.
Adeyemi trained nine weeks and did his own diving.

INSTAGRAM — Marta Silvestri

Marta Silvestri plays Renata, eight months out of a decompression injury and back in the water because the money's good.
Silvestri learned to work a full face mask in three days. Her instructor said she was the fastest he'd taught.

INSTAGRAM — Cillian Rourke

Cillian Rourke plays Sam. Twenty-three years old, first wreck, and nobody's told him what the job actually pays.

X — sound design

Most of the underwater sound in SALVAGE was recorded live. Hydrophones on the helmets, hydrophones on the hull.
Okpara spent seven months on the mix. "People assume underwater sound is muffled. It isn't. It's loud, and it's close, and it's mostly your own body."

LINKEDIN — post-festival

SALVAGE left Cascadia with the Grand Jury Prize for Direction and the prize for Best Sound.

It's the first narrative feature from Ines Kowalczyk, made with a cast who trained for nine weeks and a sound team who refused to replace anything in post.

Harbor Line opens it in twelve cities on 30 January and nationally on 6 February. We moved the date up two weeks. People came out of the festival wanting to talk about it, and we'd rather they could see it while they still want to.

INSTAGRAM — reviews

Kowalczyk still won't settle it.
