SALVAGE — PRESS KIT BIOGRAPHIES: DIRECTOR AND KEY CREW
Harbor Line Pictures

INES KOWALCZYK — Writer and Director

Long form

Ines Kowalczyk was born in Gdańsk and started out as a deckhand. She was nineteen, the work was seasonal, and she took a camera because the nights were long.

Her first documentary, Ninety Fathoms, took two years to shoot and was cut in eleven weeks. She filmed it aboard four North Sea vessels, sleeping in crew berths and paying her own way when the funding ran out twice. It premiered in 2022 and screened at thirty-one festivals. She still hasn't seen it with an audience.

SALVAGE is her first narrative feature. She wrote it with Daniel Achebe during the Ninety Fathoms edit, and she says the script came out of a single recurring conversation.

"I kept hearing the same thing from divers, which is that the water isn't the frightening part. The frightening part is the surface. Somebody up there is deciding how long you stay down, and they're doing it with a spreadsheet."

She wouldn't work with a dry set. The production filled four hundred thousand litres at a former shipyard in Ardrossan and left it full for eleven weeks. There is one shot of digital water in the finished film. It runs four seconds, and she won't say which one it is.

SALVAGE won the Grand Jury Prize for Direction at the Cascadia International Film Festival.

WREN OKPARA — Sound Designer

Long form

Wren Okpara has designed sound for nine features. He spent seven months on SALVAGE, which is longer than he's spent on anything else.

Almost all of the film's underwater sound was recorded live rather than replaced in post, which isn't how this is normally done. Okpara fixed hydrophones to the divers' helmets and to the hull of the dressed wreck, and worked inside the tank alongside the camera crew.

"People assume underwater sound is muffled. It isn't. It's loud, and it's close, and it's mostly your own body. That was the note for seven months."

He won the prize for Best Sound at Cascadia. The jury cited the film for trusting its audience to hear what it would not show them.

SUNG-MIN HA — Director of Photography

Short form, 50 words

Sung-min Ha shot SALVAGE on Alexa in underwater housings, mostly at depth in the production's tank. He lit the wreck interior with practical work lamps rigged by the diving crew, which meant every lighting change had to be made in the water by somebody wearing a helmet.
