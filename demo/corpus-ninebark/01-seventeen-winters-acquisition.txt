Ninebark Takes SEVENTEEN WINTERS, and We're Not Being Coy About Why

How many films have you seen about a job that stops existing?

We've picked up worldwide rights to SEVENTEEN WINTERS, Aino Lehtinen's documentary
about Kari Salo, who has driven the ice roads of northern Finland for 17 years and
has been told he has 2 left. The road is closing. Not because anyone decided to
close it; because the ice won't hold long enough to be worth the insurance.

Lehtinen shot it alone over 3 winters, in a truck cab, with a camera she describes
as "the cheapest thing that would survive minus 40." There's no crew in the credits
because there wasn't one. She did her own sound; you can hear the engine in every
interview, and we asked her twice whether she wanted that cleaned up. She didn't.

"People keep telling me it's a film about climate," Lehtinen said. "It's a film about
a man who is very good at something that's about to stop being a thing anyone is
good at. The ice is just the reason."

We watched it in a hotel room in November with the sound coming out of a laptop, and
we bought it before the credits finished. That almost never happens; we usually sleep
on it, argue about it, watch it again with somebody who'll disagree. Not this time.

Here's what we're not going to do. We're not going to sell you a film about the
Arctic. It's a film about work, and about being 54 and excellent and obsolete, and
if we lead with the scenery we'll bring in an audience who wants scenery and send
them home irritated. Nobody wins that.

SEVENTEEN WINTERS runs 94 minutes. It premieres at Rosehill in March and opens in
cinemas on 12 June. Lehtinen will travel with it; she's already agreed to 11 cities,
which is more than we asked for and frankly more than we'd have suggested!

Ninebark. Documentaries, and nothing else.

About Ninebark Films

Ninebark Films acquires and releases documentary features in the United Kingdom,
Ireland and North America. The company handles between 4 and 6 titles a year and has
never released a fiction film.
