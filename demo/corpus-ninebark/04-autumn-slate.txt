Our Autumn Slate Is 3 Films, and 2 of Them Are About Rooms

We looked at 240 documentaries this year. We're releasing 3 of them between September
and December, and we want to say plainly why, because "curated slate" is a phrase that
means nothing.

THE LAST TYPESETTER (3 October, 102 minutes). Sofia Bram, 2 years in a Lisbon
letterpress shop. A man, a press, and a lease that runs out.

ROOM 12 (7 November, 76 minutes). Directed by Yusuf Kaya. It takes place entirely
inside 1 room of a Nicosia hotel that has been closed since 1974 and sits inside the
buffer zone; the caretaker has kept it clean for 41 years and nobody has slept in it.
Kaya shot 300 hours and cut it to 76 minutes, which we think is the bravest thing on
this list.

FOURTH SHIFT (5 December, 88 minutes). Directed by Nell Arroway, about the night
cleaners in a Chicago hospital. It's not about heroism; it's about scheduling, and
what a rota does to a family over 6 years. You will come out angry, and we'd like
you to come out angry.

Notice what these have in common? They're small. All 3 could be described in one
sentence and dismissed in two, and every one of them is better than that description.
That's the whole difficulty of releasing documentaries, and we don't have a clever
solution to it; we just keep choosing the small ones and then working harder.

"The temptation is always to take the big one," said Marcus Ilesanmi, who runs
acquisitions here. "There's usually a big one available. It has a famous subject, it
has archive, it screens well at 11pm to a room that's had wine. And 14 months later
nobody can tell you a single frame of it. The small ones stay."

3 films. 266 minutes altogether. We'll be fine!

Ninebark. Nothing but documentaries.

About Ninebark Films

Ninebark Films acquires and releases documentary features in the United Kingdom,
Ireland and North America. The company handles between 4 and 6 titles a year and has
never released a fiction film.
