THE LAST TYPESETTER Opens 3 October, and the Trailer Has No Music in It

We had 4 cuts of this trailer. The one we're putting out is the one with nothing on
the soundtrack except a Heidelberg press and an 81-year-old man breathing.

THE LAST TYPESETTER opens in cinemas on 3 October. Rui Mendes has run the same
letterpress shop in Lisbon since 1968; his son doesn't want it, his apprentice left
in 2019, and the building's lease ends in 14 months. Director Sofia Bram spent 2
years in that shop, mostly in the corner, mostly silent.

Do you know what a composing stick sounds like? Neither did we. It's the best sound
in the film, and it's the reason we cut the music.

"I kept scoring it and un-scoring it," Bram said. "Every time I put music under Rui's
hands I was telling the audience how to feel about a man setting type, and he doesn't
need my help. He's been doing it for 56 years. It's already moving. I just had to
stop decorating it."

The trailer is 88 seconds and it went online this morning. It shows the shop, the
press, the hands, and 1 shot of the street outside. It doesn't show the ending, and
it doesn't tell you whether the shop closes; we've had that argument internally and
the answer is that you can find out in a cinema.

We'll be honest about the risk here. A silent trailer for a film about a dying trade
isn't a commercial decision, and our head of distribution said so in writing. But we
tested the scored version with 60 people and the unscored version with 60 people, and
the unscored room stayed 4 minutes longer afterwards asking questions. That's the
only metric we trust!

THE LAST TYPESETTER runs 102 minutes. Bram and Mendes will attend the London opening
on 3 October; Mendes has never been to England.

Ninebark. We only do documentaries.
