SEVENTEEN WINTERS Wins the Rosehill Grand Jury Prize, and Aino Lehtinen Wasn't There

She was driving. Actually driving, in Finland, in a truck, filming something else.

SEVENTEEN WINTERS has won the Grand Jury Prize for Documentary at the Rosehill Film
Festival. Aino Lehtinen didn't attend; she was 1,400 kilometres north of Helsinki on
the second day of a new shoot and didn't know she'd won for 9 hours.

The jury citation was 2 sentences: "A film made by one person, about one person, that
is somehow about everyone who has ever been good at something. It never once asks for
your sympathy."

We've been in this business 11 years and we've never seen a jury use the phrase
"never once asks for your sympathy," and we'd like to point out that this is exactly
what we said when we bought it. Not that we're keeping score. We're keeping score.

"I found out in a service station," Lehtinen said. "I had a coffee and I sat in the
car for a while. Then I drove for another 5 hours, because that was the plan for the
day and the light was going. Kari would think it was very funny that I was working."

Does an award change how a film like this gets released? A bit, honestly. We were
planning 11 cities and we're now planning 19; the Grand Jury Prize gets a documentary
into rooms that would otherwise say no, and pretending otherwise would be precious.
What it doesn't change is the campaign. We're still not selling the Arctic.

SEVENTEEN WINTERS runs 94 minutes and opens in cinemas on 12 June. Kari Salo has been
told about the prize. According to Lehtinen he said "good" and then went back to
checking a tyre!

Ninebark. Documentaries only, still.

About Ninebark Films

Ninebark Films acquires and releases documentary features in the United Kingdom,
Ireland and North America. The company handles between 4 and 6 titles a year and has
never released a fiction film.
