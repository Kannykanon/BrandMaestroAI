FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

THE LAST TYPESETTER Comes Home on 20 February, and the Shop Is Still Open

We can tell you now, because the film's been out for 4 months and the argument is over.

THE LAST TYPESETTER arrives on digital and disc on 20 February. Rui Mendes still has
the shop. The lease was extended by 3 years in November, 2 months after the film
opened, by a landlord who has asked us not to name him and who saw it at a Saturday
matinee in Lisbon without telling anybody.

Did the film do that? We don't know, and Sofia Bram won't claim it. Here's what we can
say: the shop had 4 commissions booked in September and it has 71 now, and a lot of
them are from people who write in the enquiry form that they saw a documentary.

"I've stopped trying to work out what I'm allowed to feel about this," Bram said. "I
made a film about something ending. It didn't end. That's a better outcome than my
film, and I'm going to have to be all right with that."

The digital release carries 26 minutes of extra material, all of it Mendes working;
there are no interviews in the extras, because we asked him for 4 and he said the
press was busy. There's also a 9-minute piece where Bram explains why she removed the
score, which is the single most requested thing from the Q&As.

What do you do with a documentary whose ending has been overtaken by events? You
release it anyway, and you let the audience hold both things at once. The trade is
still dying. This shop isn't. Both are true and the film is better for the fact that
it doesn't know that yet!

THE LAST TYPESETTER runs 102 minutes. Mendes is 82 now and has taken on an apprentice.

About Ninebark Films

Ninebark Films acquires and releases documentary features in the United Kingdom,
Ireland and North America. The company handles between 4 and 6 titles a year and has
never released a fiction film.
